Why Selling Your Home During the Holidays Is Smarter Than You Think

Every fall, I have the same conversation with sellers across Mount Pleasant, Isle of Palms, and Sullivans Island. The leaves start to turn, Thanksgiving gets closer, and suddenly the question comes up: Should we just wait until after the holidays? I understand the instinct. Life gets busy, the house is full of guests, and there's a common assumption that buyers disappear in November and December.

But that assumption has cost sellers real opportunities, and I want to push back on it.

Who Is Actually Searching for Homes in November and December?

Here is something worth thinking about. The holidays bring people to Mount Pleasant, Isle of Palms, and Sullivans Island, not just away from it. Families come in from out of state to visit for a week, sometimes two. While they are here, some of them are quietly doing exactly what you would do in a new place they love: they are driving around, checking out neighborhoods, and yes, scheduling showings.

Relocating buyers are one of the most active groups in the holiday window. Someone being transferred to Charleston for a new job in January is not waiting until spring to find a home. A retired couple who has been visiting their kids in places like Old Village or I'On for years and has finally decided to make their move permanent is not pausing their search because it is December. These buyers are motivated, they have limited time on the ground, and they want to see your home while they have the chance.

The Inventory Advantage Nobody Talks About

When other sellers pull their listings off the market in November, something interesting happens: the buyers who are still looking have fewer homes to choose from. That means your listing in Mt. Pleasant, Sullivans Island, or Isle of Palms is not competing against as many options. Less competition and serious buyers make for a combination worth paying attention to.

Historically, the buyers active during the holidays tend to be further along in their decision-making. They have typically been pre-approved, they know what they want, and they are not browsing casually. If your home is priced well against current comparables and shows well, there is a real chance you are talking to someone ready to move forward.

How to Keep Your Home Show-Ready Through the Season

I will be honest with you: holiday décor can work in your favor or against you depending on how you handle it. Tasteful decorations can make a home feel warm and lived-in, which is genuinely appealing. What you want to avoid is anything that makes rooms feel smaller, blocks natural light, or is so personalized that a buyer struggles to picture their own life there.

A few practical things that help:

  • Keep exterior lighting clean and welcoming. The days are shorter, and buyers will see your home in the dark. Good lighting matters more in winter than any other time of year.
  • Keep pathways clear and the entry clean. First impressions still happen at the front door.
  • Work with your Realtor to stay flexible on showing windows even during holiday weeks. A buyer visiting family nearby may only have a Tuesday afternoon free, and that showing matters.

Should You Take Your Home Off the Market Over the Holidays?

My answer, almost every time, is no. Taking your listing down does not pause the market. It just removes you from it. The buyers looking in December do not stop looking because your home disappeared from the search results. They simply move on to the next option.

If your home is already on the market, stay the course. If you have been thinking about listing and were waiting for January, consider this: the motivated out-of-town buyer who falls in love with Isle of Palms while visiting their daughter for Thanksgiving is not coming back in March. They are looking right now.
